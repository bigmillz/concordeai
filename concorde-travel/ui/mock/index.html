Nine directions for the interface.

The converged page first, then the second round. All nine run on the same real search — JFK to London, 18 November, 172 itineraries off the live Duffel feed, scored by the real scorer at the modelled par. Nothing here is a placeholder: every dollar figure, grade and ledger line was computed.

Concourse — the converged direction
Shortlist and Dial together, in the Concorde family’s own palette and type. A sentence you edit (round trip, one way or multi-city), the price–speed–comfort triangle with a “get me there now” preset, ten wants that charge rather than filter, a wish box you can type or speak into, advanced windows and alliances, and points balances with the transfer that beats cash. Airline logos from the feed.

05
Shortlist
Answer first. The search is a sentence; the result is three doors — cheapest all-in, fastest door to door, most comfortable — each with its grade, its price and three reasons. Everything else waits below, in your order.
Light · decisive · edit the sentence, pick a door, expand a row
06
Board
A departures board. Amber on black, split-flap cells, one row per flight; the tabs re-sort and the cells flap over. A row opens a paper receipt with the itemised ledger and a grade stamp.
Dark · evocative · switch tabs, tap a row
07
Lines
A transit map. Every journey is a line on one clock from your door to theirs — green for the train, grey for the car, blue in the air, a ring where you change planes — with the grade as a roundel. Open a line for the station-by-station receipt.
Light · spatial · hover a segment, click a line, switch the ranking
08
Dial
A triangle between price, speed and comfort. Drag the dot and the list re-ranks live with the real scorer’s numbers at 66 weightings; the price on every flight is what it costs you. The letters never move, and it says so.
Light · playful · drag, use the presets, tap a flight

The first round

01
Ledger
No cards, no boxes, no rounded corners. Pure typography, like a financial statement or a Swiss timetable. The itemised receipt is the interface.
Light · editorial · click a row to open the ledger
02
Ribbon
Results positioned on a shared clock, so a later departure really is further right. Each ribbon is the whole door-to-door trip, segmented by what you are actually doing.
Dark · spatial · hover a segment, click a row
03
Deck
One flight at a time, at magazine scale, with the grade as a huge graphic element. Nothing to scan — you look at one thing and move on.
Dark · singular · arrow keys or scroll
04
Field
The trade-off surface itself. Time against cost, every itinerary a point, coloured by grade. Drag the target and the sweep line moves — the grades do not.
Dark · instrument · drag the slider, click a dot

One thing you will notice and should not read as a bug. Some inventory is synthetic — this came from a Duffel test token, so a few itineraries are invented, and one cluster shares an identical departure time across four carriers that each claim to operate themselves. The fictional carrier has been filtered out here; the codeshare oddity is the test feed, not the adapter. The grades are real: par is modelled for the route and the month, and the letters run from A to F the way a real search does.
