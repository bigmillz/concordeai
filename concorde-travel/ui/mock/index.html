ConcordeGo

Four directions for the interface.

All four run on the same real search — JFK to London, 18 November, 145 itineraries off the live Duffel feed, scored by the real scorer. Nothing here is a placeholder: every dollar figure, grade and ledger line was computed.

01
Ledger
No cards, no boxes, no rounded corners. Pure typography, like a financial statement or a Swiss timetable. The itemised receipt is the interface.
Light · editorial · click a row to open the ledger
02
Ribbon
Results positioned on a shared clock, so a later departure really is further right. Each ribbon is the whole door-to-door trip, segmented by what you are actually doing.
Dark · spatial · hover a segment, click a row
03
Deck
One flight at a time, at magazine scale, with the grade as a huge graphic element. Nothing to scan — you look at one thing and move on.
Dark · singular · arrow keys or scroll
04
Field
The trade-off surface itself. Time against cost, every itinerary a point, coloured by grade. Drag the target and the sweep line moves — the grades do not.
Dark · instrument · drag the slider, click a dot

Two things you will notice and should not read as bugs. Almost everything grades A+ — route par is still the un-calibrated $1,050, so a genuinely cheap route saturates the top of the scale. That is the open decision from last night, and these mockups make the case for settling it better than the histogram did. The Every grade tab shows the real range. Some inventory is synthetic — this came from a Duffel test token, so a few itineraries are invented, and one cluster shares an identical departure time across four carriers that each claim to operate themselves. The fictional carrier has been filtered out here; the codeshare oddity is the test feed, not the adapter.
