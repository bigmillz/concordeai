## q30 — Quantization explained

**Q:** Explain what 4-bit quantization does to an LLM and when it actually matters. Keep it short and technical.

*28.88s, 2227 chars, models: Gemma 4 26B, searched: False, drafts: *

4-bit quantization maps high-precision weights (typically **FP16** or **BF16**) to a 4-bit integer space to reduce the VRAM footprint by roughly **4x** and increase inference throughput.

In a 16-bit model, each parameter takes 2 bytes of VRAM. Moving to 4-bit reduces that to **0.5 bytes** per parameter (plus a small overhead for metadata), allowing a 70B parameter model to fit into roughly **40GB of VRAM** rather than requiring the **140GB** needed for full precision.

## The Mechanism: Mapping and Scaling
Because a s-bit range only allows for 16 discrete values, you cannot simply round a floating-point number without losing massive amounts of information. Modern techniques like **GPTQ** or **AWQ** use "group-wise" scaling to mitigate this.

The process involves:
- **Scaling Factors**: Dividing weights by a constant to fit the distribution into the 4-bit range.
- **Group sizes**: Applying different scales to specific blocks of weights (e.g., every 64 or 128 weights) so that the rounding error is localized and doesn't destroy the model's overall distribution.

## When It Matters
The trade-off is between **VRAM capacity** and **perplexity** (the mathematical measure of how much the model's accuracy degrades).

**It matters when:**
- **Hardware is the bottleneck**: If you need to run a model that is 40GB on a 24GB GPU, 4-bit quantization is the only way to fit the weights into memory.
- **Inference throughput is the goal**: Smaller weights require less bandwidth to move from VRAM to the compute cores, which can significantly increase **tokens-per-second**.

**It matters less when:**
- **You have massive headroom**: If you are running a small model on an 80GB A100, there is no benefit to quantizing; you should run the highest precision available to preserve intelligence.
- **The model is small**: Models with very low parameter counts (under 7B) tend to degrade significantly when pushed to 4-bit because they lack the "parameter redundancy" larger models use to absorb quantization errors.

The "sweet spot" is currently **4-bit to 6-bit**. Going below 4-bit (to 2-bit or 3-bit) typically causes an "intelligence collapse" where the model loses the ability to reason or follow complex instructions.
