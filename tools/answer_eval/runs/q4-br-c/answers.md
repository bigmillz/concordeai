## q30 — Quantization explained

**Q:** Explain what 4-bit quantization does to an LLM and when it actually matters. Keep it short and technical.

*29.45s, 2723 chars, models: Gemma 4 26B, searched: False, drafts: *

4-bit quantization compresses the weights of a Large Language Model from their native precision (typically **FP16** or **BF16**) down to a 4-bit integer representation to reduce the memory footprint and increase inference speed.

At its core, quantization maps a wide range of floating-point values to a much smaller discrete set of values. In a 4-bit setup, you only have **16 possible states** ($2^4$) to represent the weight of a single parameter. Because 16 states cannot capture the nuance of a high-precision distribution, the process involves calculating a **scale factor** (and often a zero-point) for blocks of weights to minimize the rounding error between the original value and the quantized value.

## The Mechanics

The primary goal is to maximize **information density**. Since weights in a neural network often follow a distribution centered around zero, grouping weights into "blocks" (e.g., 32 or 64 weights per scale factor) allows the quantization to maintain the relative shape of the distribution while fitting into a much smaller bit-depth.

Modern techniques like **GGUF (K-Quants)** or **EXL2** use sophisticated importance-based rescaling to ensure that the most critical weights—those that impact the output most heavily—suffer less precision loss.

## When It Matters

The decision to use 4-bit quantization is usually driven by hardware constraints, but the trade-off is always between **VRAM capacity** and **perplexity** (the measure of how well the model predicts the next token).

*   **Hardware constraints:** If you want to run a **70B parameter model** on consumer hardware, 4-bit is the threshold. A 70B model in FP16 requires ~140GB of VRAM; at 4-bit, it fits in ~40GB. This allows you to run massive models on multi-GPU setups (like two RTX 3090/4090s) that would otherwise be impossible.
*   **Inference Latency:** 4-bit weights are smaller, meaning less data needs to be moved from VRAM to the GPU cores during every calculation cycle. This results in higher **tokens per second (TPS)**.
*   **The "Perplexity Wall":** For models with smaller parameter counts (e.g., under 7B-13B), 4-bit quantization can cause noticeable "intelligence" degradation, where the model loses coherence or reasoning ability. For larger models (30B+), the loss in perplexity is often negligible to a human observer, making 4-bit the "sweet spot" for local deployment.
